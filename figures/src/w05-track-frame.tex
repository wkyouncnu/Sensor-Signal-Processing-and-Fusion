    $a = \pi_p + 90^\circ$ and the second does, because
    $\cos(\pi_p+90^\circ) = -\sin\pi_p$ and $\sin(\pi_p+90^\circ) = \cos\pi_p$.};

\begin{scope}[shift={(86,-8)}]
  \node[ttl, anchor=north west] at (-8,44) {reading components is multiplying by a rotation};

  \node[blurb, anchor=north west, text width=88mm] at (-8,37) {%
    The dot product of a vector with a \emph{unit} vector is the length of its
    shadow on that direction. Both components are therefore one dot product
    each, taken against the pair drawn in panel (3):};
  \node[anchor=north west, font=\small] at (-8,18) {%
    $x_e^{\,p} = \hat{\mathbf{t}}^{\!\top}\big(\mathbf{p}^{\,n}-\mathbf{p}_i^{\,n}\big),
     \qquad
     y_e^{\,p} = \hat{\mathbf{n}}^{\!\top}\big(\mathbf{p}^{\,n}-\mathbf{p}_i^{\,n}\big)$};
  \node[blurb, anchor=north west, text width=88mm] at (-8,9) {%
    Stacked, the two dot products are the two rows of one matrix, and that
    matrix is a rotation:};
  \node[anchor=north west, font=\small] at (-8,-2) {%
    $\begin{bmatrix} x_e^{\,p} \\ y_e^{\,p} \end{bmatrix}
      = \mathbf{R}_p^{\,n}(\pi_p)^{\!\top}
        \begin{bmatrix} x^n-x_i^n \\ y^n-y_i^n \end{bmatrix},
      \qquad
      \mathbf{R}_p^{\,n}(\pi_p)
      = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\pi_p & -\sin\pi_p \\ \sin\pi_p & \ \ \cos\pi_p \end{bmatrix}$};
  \node[blurb, anchor=north west, text width=88mm] at (-8,-24) {%
    The columns of $\mathbf{R}_p^{\,n}(\pi_p)$ are $\hat{\mathbf{t}}$ and
    $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$, so that matrix carries path-frame values \emph{into}
    north and east. The traffic in panel (2) runs the other way, which is why
    the transpose appears --- and for a rotation
    $\mathbf{R}^{-1} = \mathbf{R}^{\!\top}$, so nothing is ever inverted.};
\end{scope}
